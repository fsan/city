# Measured stop arrivals and regularity

Bounded implementation plan: record a visit only when a current, non-retiring service bus physically finishes an approach to its target stop. Deployment dwell, clearance and relief-only junctions do not qualify. Maintain current and most recent retired route/window observations per line, keyed by route version and node; never merge route revisions. Report visits, latest arrival, interval sample count, latest/mean/min/max intervals and live waiting counts through scalar ABI and Transport Authority.

Intervals use simulation seconds. Daytime intervals crossing closed hours are omitted; all-day intervals cross midnight normally. Coverage changes start a separate observation record. No timetable, compliance score, payment changes, penalties or permanent tests.

## Implemented semantics

A visit occurs at the fixed-step transition from a traversed street approach into the bus's target-stop dwell. This is the existing simulation's stop-admission point, with the vehicle body still set back from the junction for clearance. It does not require boarding, an empty seat or a waiting passenger; full buses still count. The bus must belong to the active line's current route and operator, be within coverage hours and not retiring. Starting at a deployment stop does not count, even though existing deployment dwell can board passengers and earn delivery. Completing a clearance segment or stopping only for driver relief does not count. Relief coinciding with an actual target-stop arrival counts the service visit once.

Every line retains two fixed records of up to 16 stops: current route/window and the most recent retired route/window. Each stores its version, coverage and ordered stable node IDs, plus per-stop visit count, latest absolute simulation timestamp, eligible interval count, last/mean/min/max interval. No visits use timestamp -1; no eligible intervals use -1 statistics. Two visits are sufficient for one eligible interval, not evidence of perfect or reliable service. Means weight eligible intervals equally. Simultaneous genuine arrivals are separate visits and can produce a zero interval.

Applying even an identical route starts a new version and archives the previous record immediately, including while paused. Withdrawal archives the route and clears the current record; slot reuse does not merge it back. A subsequent route/coverage change replaces the single retired record. A coverage change is detected at the next transport update before any arrivals are recorded. Operator/fleet changes alone retain route/window observations: these describe line service across interventions, not operator-specific performance. Lane changes and street splits keep the record because stop-node identity and ordered route remain unchanged; observed intervals can therefore reflect changed travel conditions. No causal attribution is claimed.

Daytime intervals are eligible only when both arrivals belong to the same 06:00–22:00 window. The first arrival after a closure increments visits and latest time but creates no interval from the prior day. Existing eligible statistics remain: the last eligible interval may be older than the latest arrival. All-day intervals include midnight and driver shift changes. Hours with no completed arrival pair do not manufacture a zero or a missed-service score. Records contain completed intervals only, not a forecast, resident waiting-duration history or headway promise.

The Transport Authority has a collapsible report with current/retired selection, coverage and off-hours labels, session clock, stop locate buttons, visit/sample counts and last/mean/range. Current waiting counts include residents physically waiting to board that line/version at that node; retired records show unavailable, never live values. Residents' individual waiting duration remains in their inspector. Only these two records per line survive within a session; Refresh/Restart clears them.

## Limits / next work

This is descriptive aggregate observation, with one retired record per line and no arrival event log, rolling trend, operator attribution, waiting-duration distribution, timetable or regularity penalty. Existing payment formulas, dispatch, boarding, resident routing and driver/account rules are unchanged. Any future contractual regularity target requires its own explicit design and authorization; do not infer penalties from these measurements.
