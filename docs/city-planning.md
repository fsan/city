# Streets, parcels and zoning

The seeded city occupies roughly ninety irregular enclosed blocks over a 1,320 × 1,040 metre plan, with several parcels per block. Population remains 3,840. Streets are connected segments at arbitrary angles, including curved corridors. The local mesh carries a small wobble rather than wide jitter: a run that wanders nine metres off its line misses the cross street it was aimed at and leaves a dead end, while a two-and-a-half metre wobble still closes a face. Buildings and addresses are independent of grid positions.

N opens road construction. Choose straight (start/end) or curved (start/control/end), click the map, review the coloured preview and price, then Build or Enter. Escape discards the draft. Endpoints snap to nodes or street interiors; intersections split existing segments and connect automatically. Construction costs £25/metre immediately. Roads must connect to the existing network and cannot cross occupied buildings, parks, protected work orders, steep terrain, overlapping roads or very shallow junctions. No bridges, tunnels, bulldozer or construction crews yet. Limits: 640 nodes and 1,200 segments. Failure leaves graph and treasury untouched.

Stable node, building and street identifiers preserve bus stops, addresses and inspectors. Splitting a street retains its old segment ID for the first part, inherits lane allocation for the second and reconnects in-flight travellers. New streets receive numbered names and vacant frontage parcels. Enclosed blocks are traced from graph edges; zoning is stored on parcels so block changes do not silently change their permitted use.

Z shows zoning. Select a parcel, choose residential, commercial, industrial, mixed, civic/park reserve or unzoned, and apply to that parcel or its enclosed block. Open roadside frontages can be zoned individually. Existing buildings remain when rezoned. This increment defines permitted future use; it does not yet simulate developer demand or automatic construction. Road-frontage parcel packing is deliberately simple and axis aligned; redevelopment and parcel subdivision remain future work.

F toggles pedestrian density independently of G traffic. Counts include walkers and people waiting outside, excluding riders, cyclists and people inside buildings. Road inspectors show counts and people per 100 m² of sidewalk, using 1.9 × segment length as the sidewalk-area estimate. Heat clouds aggregate by world location and broaden when zoomed out. Sidewalk selection responds to destination side with individual variation; both sides are used. Full intersection crossing phases and collision physics remain future work.

Verification: ReleaseSafe Docker build; JavaScript syntax and diff checks; rendered city inspection. Temporary WASM checks exercised straight/curved construction, treasury debits, new frontage parcels, block zoning, protected buildings, disconnected-road rejection, a live midpoint junction split and passenger conservation. A 320-second run completed 9,505 trips with 381,924 maximum sampled vertices. A separate sidewalk sample counted 352 and 329 walkers on opposite sides, with all 23 bus riders accounted for. No permanent test suite was added.
