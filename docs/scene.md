# Current scene

`city-planning.md` supersedes the grid layout and frontage assumptions below: 34 irregular blocks, multiple parcels per block, dynamic road/node counts and separate resident destination-building IDs. The old notes are retained as historical context.

# Scene scheme

X/Z are the ground plane; Y is elevation, in abstract metres. Bellwether has 18 × 16 authored lots (288), 12 neighbourhoods, 323 street nodes and 610 bidirectional segments over 360 × 320 metres. The fixed ASCII plan in `src/scene/city.zig` is the source of building uses. No random generator is involved.

Terrain is an authored height function: two eastward rises (8 m and 11 m) and a southern terrace (+5 m), joined by slopes. Buildings use level foundations at their footprint's highest corner. Front paths follow terrain and end at supported steps near the uphill corner of each building. Residents walk these connectors; cars are stored at street nodes. Parks follow the terrain without foundations. Ground and road triangles split at the exact terrain crease coordinates — x 30, 210, 1140 and 1300, and z 130, 830 and 1020 — so no tile chords across a corner the ground actually turns at and every drawn surface matches the height the simulation walks on. Residents are hidden while dwelling inside buildings or riding vehicles; detailed interiors are not modelled.

Street records contain endpoints, 3D length, absolute grade, district, condition and permissions. A shared all-pairs next-hop table minimises length × (1 + 3 × grade) / (0.7 + condition / 100). It refreshes every 60 simulation seconds and after order completion/cancellation. Existing travellers finish their segment before taking a new next hop. Temporary works slow walking by 35% and vehicles by 55%; rerouting around temporary disruption is not yet included in route estimates.

Building-to-node links and the reverse entry lookup are explicit. Residents have home/employer IDs, current/next/destination nodes, travel time, frontage movement phase and optional work-order assignment. Ordinary commuters have shortened waits and a simple day/night preference. Crews override their routines until released. These are adult placeholders, not full household schedules.

Rendering consumes scene and simulation data without advancing them. Geometry is batched into a single depth-tested vertex stream: XYZ clip coordinates plus RGB. Residents remain one six-vertex upright quad each. Selected residents show their route in gold; active crews wear amber. The orthographic camera supports rotation, pan, cursor-centred zoom and report-driven focus. Bounds and depth range derive from town dimensions. Picking intersects elevated building boxes and checks visible resident/car screen positions against the nearest building depth. Selected trips mark their actual departure node/property in blue and destination in amber. The river is one strip with a shared bisector edge at each centreline point, so consecutive spans meet exactly at a bend, and the strip reaches past the waterline so its own boundary is buried under the carved bank: the water ends wherever the bank rises through its plane, and the two surfaces cannot cut through each other.

The current buffer holds 600,000 vertices; this town produces roughly 435,000, with some variation for route highlighting. Geometry is still rebuilt each frame. Profile before expanding further; cached static world meshes and GPU camera transforms are the next rendering optimisations. Residents, buildings, roads and orders retain stable array IDs for the session.

`transport.md` describes vehicle queues, bus routes, frontage departures, travel choices and lane allocation. Cars and buses use the same street graph; directional queues and bus lanes are additional transport state.


The deterministic suburban pass leaves 46 home parcels vacant and reduces peripheral home height. Home assignment weights taller homes more heavily, preserving 3,840 residents while keeping suburbs less populated. This creates space, not a construction tool. Building values include a 0.9–1.1 sunlight multiplier from a southern-neighbour height proxy. Windows have side/top clearance on all four facades, with fewer north-facing windows on shaded properties. Sun movement changes face brightness and approximate ground-only shadows between 06:00 and 18:00; this is not ray-traced solar access or night lighting.

A separate walking next-hop table adds seven cost units for an unmarked junction crossing. Four fifths of walkers use that preference and align to the positive-side marked crossing; the remainder use the original direct routes. Unmarked crossings remain possible. Markings are installed at both ends of a selected segment. Seed rules use nearby civic/park destinations and occupied neighbourhood counts, applied once; player edits are never reseeded.
